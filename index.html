    How the capture modes compare

    Every row is the same panorama, photographed a different way. Seam slide is how far an object standing on the ground jumps between one frame and the next, which is the defect a crop cannot fix. Nothing here is a score: the mode with no slide at all also flattens the distance.

    Synthetic preview

    Render the plan against a made-up scene, so the framing can be checked without flying anything.

    A crop selects pixels; it cannot change the projection they were captured under, so neighbouring strips disagree wherever the cut falls. Reprojecting resamples each strip onto the viewer's curve, which lines the ground up exactly at every seam. Anything standing above the ground still slides — see the comparison above for how far.

    On the map

    Place the flight on real ground and see what each frame covers. Needs no account and no API key — the street map is OpenStreetMap, the satellite imagery is Esri, and both are free to use.

    Camera poses, for reconstructing the scene

    A crop cannot remove the parallax of anything standing above the ground, because the ray the panorama needs exists in no photograph. The way out is to reconstruct the scene from the real photographs and render that ray — and once a reconstruction exists, extra frames are free, so the finest capture density above costs nothing to render.

    These are the planned poses, not measured ones. The aircraft hovers near them, not on them. Run ordinary structure-from-motion on your photographs first; use this model to align the result to the plan and to render the panorama along the curve.
